# Persistent pawns beat stateless prompts on the Rim

By late 2026 the field of LLM-driven RimWorld mods is crowded but shallow. One dialogue mod, **RimTalk, has about 142k Workshop subscribers**, roughly ten times its nearest rival, and most of its depth comes from add-ons with 20k to 77k subscribers each. **Every dialogue mod surveyed builds a fresh prompt per event**: none keeps a persistent agent per pawn with its own identity and memory outside the game. The agent-bridge world is the reverse. At least eight MCP/HTTP bridges appeared in 2026 and the bridge is now a commodity. The most serious project, zorrobyte/rimagent, runs almost HermesVoice's stack (a local 27B with speculative decoding) and reports that most failures come from the interface rather than the model. Outside RimWorld, research simulations, the Skyrim mods (Mantella, CHIM, SkyrimNet) and a few shipped games agree on a recipe: per-character first-person memory limited to what the character witnessed; a fixed bio paired with an evolving one; inner state held as numbers outside the LLM; and LLM output narrowed to text plus a small closed set of effects that the game validates. Three things matter most for HermesVoice. Its external, persistent, per-pawn agent fills a real gap. Its effects must stay a closed enum with magnitudes set in C#, not by the model. And its easy engine is a clear differentiator, because **no RimWorld or Skyrim LLM mod ships its own engine**. Several of the keeper's fixed decisions carry measurable risks: port 8765 is shared by at least three existing bridges, n-gram speculation gives about 0% on varied chat with exactly this Gemma 4 26B MoE, and Gemma's sliding-window attention limits KV reuse when pawn slots are leased. Each risk has a cheap mitigation, set out below.

## RimTalk's 142k subscribers set the bar, but its add-ons carry the depth

RimTalk (Workshop 3551203752) had **141,634 current subscribers and 210,523 lifetime** on 2026-11-24, an update dated 2026-11-20, and support for RimWorld 1.5 to 1.7 ([Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3551203752); [GitHub](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk)). Its pipeline has four steps. A Harmony patch intercepts vanilla social interactions; a context builder runs on the main thread; the LLM call runs on a background task; parsing and display happen back on the main thread, behind "generation tokens so stale async callbacks can't overwrite newer state" ([RimTalk](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk)). Triggers include social interactions, thoughts (with a random 1 to 2.5 day cooldown per thought), battles, skill-ups, injuries, sleep and wake, letters, quests, player-typed lines and a fallback chitchat timer. The context covers almost everything a pawn has: traits, backstory, genes, ideology, skills, health, mood, recent thoughts, relations, gear, room, weather and wealth. Code caps keep it small: three traits, three thoughts, five ranked events, three pawns and two exchanges of history. Output is streamed JSONL `{name,text}` built from Scriban templates the player can edit in game. **Its default cloud model is `gemma-4-26b-a4b-it`**, the same model HermesVoice has chosen, so RimTalk on Gemini is the natural baseline for voice quality ([RimTalk](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk)).

The depth lives in RimTalk's add-on ecosystem. **Event+ has 76,730 subscribers.** It keeps raid and quest facts in context for the event's whole duration because, without it, "that original raid info scrolls out of context, and the model starts guessing" ([Event+](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3612632140)). Expand Memory (61,001) adds four-layer memory with decay and relevance scoring ([Expand Memory](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3608181242)). Persona Director (50,594) generates personas from roles, genes, passions and dead or hostile relatives, adds "Persona Evolution", and gives the player a per-character notes box ([Persona Director](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3619548407)). Lucid Chronicle (4,941, June 2026) has each pawn write a daily first-person summary that feeds a colony chronicle, and can route that work to a separate summary model ([Lucid Chronicle](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3749797638)). The audience's revealed preferences are plain: **players install event grounding, memory and persona depth on top of the base mod**, which is what HermesVoice wants to build in.

RimTalk's pacing is conservative. It keeps one request in flight globally, starts a talk every 10 s by default, times out after 60 s, and stops at 3x/4x speed. Requests expire by type: player requests never, urgent ones after 5 s, chitchat after 20 s, events after 5,000 ticks. Speakers are chosen by a per-persona "chattiness" weight of 0.15 to 0.95 and skipped when nothing about them has changed ([RimTalk](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk)). Memory injection is off by default. When enabled, each pawn keeps up to 25 episodes with a 4-day half-life (7 days for trauma), saved on a hidden hediff. Effects are also off by default; when on, a closed set {Insult, Slight, Chat, Kind} maps to thoughts worth ±3 mood and opinion for one day, stacking to three ([RimTalk](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk)). The project also published a design log with two lessons worth copying outright. **Keep the trigger beside each past line**: dialogue-only history made monologues loop and arguments "reset tone". **Strip ambient state before saving history**, because pawns "kept complaining about dead bodies hours later" ([RimTalk docs](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk)).

Reception sits mostly in Steam comments and in Japanese and Russian coverage. It is dominated by plumbing and grounding, not prose. Users complain that pawns call out "to someone in another room from miles away", that the game "leaves no space for local generation", and that "Local is pretty much dead for since 1.7". They report a "FF>FF>FF" output loop, reasoning models exhausting `max_tokens`, and Hulks described as "slender and frail" ([RimTalk comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/3551203752)). Small local models under about 8B fail the strict JSON format ([Local RimTalk guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3779985741)). No readable source said RimTalk colonists "all sound the same", and none complained about purple prose. That absence is weak evidence either way, because Reddit and the Ludeon forums could not be searched.

The other dialogue mods each push one axis further. **RimMind** is a ten-repo suite from one author: a core runtime with a layered context engine and tool-call registry, plus memory, personality, storyteller and bridge modules. It forces output through a native tool call, `express_dialogue{speech, thought_tag, thought_desc, relation_delta}`, with a repair parser and a sanitizer that strips `<think>` blocks, fences and raw JSON. Its system prompt asks for 15 to 40 character lines, "no polite positivity when suffering", and no stage directions. Its cadence curve, pair quotas and coalescing keys are the most careful pacing in the field ([RimMind org](https://github.com/RimWorld-RimMind-Mod)). It has **only 760 Workshop subscribers**, and the Workshop copy lags GitHub by seven months ([Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3707742321)). **EchoColony** (12,015) centres on player-initiated chat, including group chats and chats with animals, mechs, factions and the storyteller. It lets the model invoke drastic actions: mental breaks, recruitment, resurrection, trait changes ([EchoColony](https://github.com/CarlosNahuelcoy/EchoColony)). Its users say colonists feel alike without a personality mod, and local Ollama models drop character after a few turns ([discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/discussion/3463505750/600780465871269721)). **RimChat** (16,247) gives comms-console faction leaders who "can trigger raids, aid drops, and quests". Its author warns that a promise made "without explicit notice" is the model "making things up" ([Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3683001105); [mirror](https://catalogue.smods.ru/archives/444459)). **AIColonists** (remexre) lets the LLM choose only among impulses that C# has already verified as reachable, and estimates **about 60k tokens per colonist per game day** ([AIColonists](https://github.com/remexre/AIColonists)).

## Narrators that restrain themselves outdraw agents that play

RimGPT (6,646 subscribers, updated 2026-11-17) is the long-running voiced narrator. Harmony patches feed prioritised "phrases" into per-persona buffers, and each persona speaks every 25 to 60 s through Azure TTS, skipping a turn when at most one phrase is new. Its only memory is a summary the model rewrites on each call within a word cap. **Its two default personas hear each other** and banter: "Brrainz", whispering, is "sceptical about everything Tynan says" ([RimGPT](https://github.com/pardeike/RimGPT)). It has no game effects by design, and its README says local models make "the experience worse". EchoColony's storyteller chat waits at least 120 s between comments, ignores repeats for 10 minutes, and lets deaths and major threats skip the cooldown. It strips `[TRIGGER]` tags from comment prompts so commentary can never fire an event ([PR #41](https://github.com/CarlosNahuelcoy/EchoColony/pull/41)).

LLM storytellers that choose events are more popular than LLM agents that play. **Rimteller has 4,245 subscribers.** At checkpoints it sends the colony status and "all triggerable events" to the model, which returns one event name plus its reasoning. Events "with their own internal logic" bypass the AI ([Rimteller](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3638662307)). The most popular agent-style mods are an order of magnitude smaller: RimAgent Orca 553, RIMAPI 541, RimMCP 206. The newest storytellers split work into a director call (`no_event`, `vanilla_incident`, or a custom Lua event) and a writer call that composes multi-stage events from 42 actions, with at most 6 concurrent events and expiry after 5 idle days ([CustomEventByAI](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3841839281)). Others use rolling "but/therefore" beat arcs ([fable-teller](https://github.com/newcarrotgames/fable-teller)). Two more points belong in HermesVoice's narrator design. Diaries and memoirs are a recurring niche: Pawn Diary has 566 subscribers, and RimMemoir and RimLegend are similar ([Pawn Diary](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3752041535)). And a commenter said an older daily-report mod produced "report after report... repetitive useless info" ([mirror](https://catalogue.smods.ru/archives/323531)).

### Bridges are a commodity, and 8765 is already taken

**RIMAPI** is the most-used bridge (541 subscribers, v1.12.0 on 2026-11-04). It runs a REST server with about 170 routes plus an SSE event stream, and drains a locked queue of up to 10 requests per Unity frame ([RIMAPI](https://github.com/IlyaChichkov/RIMAPI)). It has no auth, no rate limits and no permission toggles, and its CORS reflects any origin with credentials. Any web page open in a local browser can therefore kill or resurrect pawns. **It listens on `localhost:8765`.** So does Sorcerio's Antfarm ([Antfarm](https://github.com/Sorcerio/Rimworld-Antfarm)), and so does zorrobyte/rimbridge, which serves `POST /rpc` and `/events?since=` on 127.0.0.1:8765 ([rimbridge](https://github.com/zorrobyte/rimbridge)). HermesVoice's default port therefore collides with the most popular bridge and with the bridge behind the most capable agent. RLE, a benchmark built on RIMAPI, is a different kind of warning. The README of its N=1 board says "**0 of 12 harness/model rows beat the unmanaged baseline**" of vanilla pawn AI ([RLE](https://github.com/AppSprout-dev/RLE)). LLM control of a colony does not obviously make it survive longer. The value case is story and companionship.

**zorrobyte/rimagent** (69 stars, v2.5.0 on 2026-11-22) is the system closest to HermesVoice. It runs "Qwen 3 27B (NVFP4, speculative decoding) served by vLLM with thinking enabled and native tool calls". Its bridge marshals work onto a `Root.Update` postfix, which runs in menus and while paused, under a per-frame time budget, and "errors become JSON, never Unity exceptions". The model schedules its own next wake with `end_turn(notes, wake_in_hours, wake_on)`, floored at 8 game hours in calm. "Calm steps run with the game at 3×; urgent steps pause it." Urgent events arriving mid-step are injected after the next tool call ([PAPER](https://github.com/zorrobyte/rimagent/blob/main/docs/PAPER.md)). Across 6,335 tool calls it measured **a median of 24 calls and 80 s per think step**, with 10.8% of calls erroring, mostly on guessed identifiers. Its interface defects are lessons for any HTTP companion. "Pausing to think looked frozen to a human." Modal dialogs paused the game invisibly. Models passed JSON as strings and used aliased parameter names. "A hung LLM socket with a 15-minute timeout froze play for half an hour." **"Each became a harness rule rather than a prompt instruction."** Its v2 adds a deterministic in-mod "Steward" layer, after which the agent deleted 416 lines of its own reflex watchers. Its authors conclude that "a reflex that must round-trip through an HTTP poll loop at 2 Hz is also strictly worse than one running on the simulation tick" ([PAPER §8.2](https://github.com/zorrobyte/rimagent/blob/main/docs/PAPER.md)).

The other bridges add a few patterns worth adopting. Pardeike's RimBridgeServer, by the author of Harmony, gives every reply an operation id, status, duration and the current game `tick`. It warns about unknown arguments by listing the valid ones, and uses a per-run bridge token ([RimBridgeServer](https://github.com/pardeike/RimBridgeServer)). RimAgent (mushroomTW) offers Confirm, Execute, Auto and Full modes; in Auto, a classifier asks how hard an action is to reverse and whether the player requested it. Confirmed calls replay exactly without asking the model again ([RimAgent](https://github.com/mushroomTW/RimAgent)). allenmonkey970's rimworld-mcp shows the main pitfall: requests that time out stay queued and run anyway, so a retried command can execute twice ([rimworld-mcp](https://github.com/allenmonkey970/rimworld-mcp)). One 1-star repo, Bayarddevries/rimworld-mcp, was "built for Hermes agent". It has an HTTP API, an SSE stream and an event watcher that posts to Telegram, so external persistent agents for *playing* the colony do exist ([repo](https://github.com/Bayarddevries/rimworld-mcp)). **None of the more than 80 repos and 30 Workshop items found gives each pawn's voice a persistent external agent.**

## Skyrim, Smallville and shipped games converge on one recipe

The research reference design is still Stanford's Generative Agents. Retrieval scores each memory as recency (a decay of 0.995 per game hour) plus importance (an LLM rating from 1 to 10, set when the memory is written) plus embedding relevance, with equal weights. Reflection fires when summed importance passes 150, about 2 to 3 times a game day. In the ablation, believability fell from 29.89 with the full architecture to 26.88 without reflection and 21.21 without memory, planning or reflection ([Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)). The same paper documents the failures HermesVoice must design against. Agents were overly formal ("It was good talking to you as always"). Isabella "rarely said no". Others embellished: Yuriko made her neighbour Adam Smith the author of *Wealth of Nations*. Gossip emerged with no gossip module: news of a party spread from 1 agent to 13 in two game days, and only 1.3% of answers hallucinated a relationship. The cheaper follow-ups matter more for a local GPU. Lyfe Agents cost about **$0.50 per agent-hour against an estimated ~$25 for Smallville**, through summarize-and-forget memory, an asynchronous self-summary, and "options" that end on non-LLM checks ([Lyfe](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02172v1)). Affordable Generative Agents compresses each pair's history into Relationship, Feeling and Summary-Events fields ([AGA](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)). Humanoid Agents adds a "System 1" of needs, emotions and 0-30 closeness bands ([Humanoid Agents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05418)). Project Sid's cognitive controller broadcasts one decision to both the talk and action modules, which prevents "says X, does Y" ([Project Sid](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)).

The Skyrim mods are the nearest consumer analogue. **SkyrimNet** gives each NPC a private memory store with importance and decay, written from the NPC's own point of view. NPCs "know only what they can see, hear or reasonably infer". Bios can be static or "dynamic event-driven", and **an AI bio update is previewed as a diff** ([SkyrimNet features](https://goncalo22.github.io/SkyrimNet-GamePlugin/Features/overview)). SkyrimNet also splits work across seven model roles: dialogue, memory generation, profile generation, action evaluation, combat chatter, a fast "meta" model for speaker selection and memory queries, and a diary ([model agents](https://goncalo22.github.io/SkyrimNet-GamePlugin/Large%20Language%20Models/model-agents)). CHIM keeps witness-only memory in PostgreSQL, writes first-person diaries, and split its bios into static and dynamic parts in "Dynamic Profiles 2.0" ([Nexus](https://nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/126330)). Its installer is a WSL2 distro of about 849 MB, which shows how heavy setup can become. Mantella targets LLM replies under 0.5 s. Its v0.14 (April 2026) added groups of up to five NPCs who gather instead of shouting, with NPC-initiated talk off by default ([Mantella](https://art-from-the-machine.github.io/Mantella/); [PC Gamer via shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2026/04/27/182997/ii-mod-dlia-skyrim-polucil-krupnoe-obnovlenie-i-naucil-npc-pomnit-razgovory-i-zamecat-mir-vokrug-nix)). It was still called "stiff", and its docs admit small LLMs ignore "don't describe actions" ([Mantella Q&A](https://art-from-the-machine.github.io/Mantella/pages/issues_qna.html)).

Shipped games add consequence and restraint. In Where Winds Meet, NPCs track an affinity tied to a personal problem, and LLM output is limited to text plus a fixed flag set. Players still beat it by echoing an NPC's own lines back to it ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/where-winds-meet-and-ai-how-the-mmos-chatbot-npcs-work/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/where-winds-meet-ai-npc-llm-chatgpt-steam-2000650074)). PUBG Ally keeps a behaviour-tree reflex layer that never waits on the LLM, treats tool results as "the only ground truth", and remembers the player's preferred weapons across matches, which playtesters liked ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/)). inZOI shows a character's inner thoughts in real time ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-au/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-naraka-bladepoint-inzoi-launch-this-month/)). Stardew's ValleyTalk lets the LLM take over only the repetitive filler and keeps hand-written lines for events ([Nexus](https://nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/30319)). Reception repeats the same complaints across systems: "hollow" tone ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/where-winds-meet/reception-ai-chatbots)), "kinda just looks like regular NPC AI" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/the-sims-rival-inzoi-uses-special-nvidia-ai-tech-to-power-its-characters-but-this-kinda-just-looks-like-regular-npc-ai-to-me)), and Suck Up! at 62% positive ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726370/Suck_Up/)). The Dwarf Fortress audience, closest in temperament to RimWorld's, pushed back on "AI-finessed prose" ([Steam DF](https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3716062978739837554)). The lesson for a colony sim: **the LLM should remember and react to simulated events, not invent them.**

### Distinct, plain-spoken voices come from examples, samplers and less memory

Out of the box, LLM role-play voices are hard to tell apart. On a speaker-verification test, real character pairs scored 72.91 on distinction while CharacterLLM scored 38.98, attributed to "a built-in LLM style that persists across roles" ([arXiv 2405.10150](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10150v2)). **That matters for the two-slot design: two separate agents on the same weights share that house style**, so separate slots alone do not create distinct voices. Four levers have evidence behind them. First, short concrete examples beat rules. In the CPDC NPC challenge, a "respond naturally and concisely, don't exaggerate" instruction plus two-turn few-shot examples raised the score from 0.510 to 0.601, while chain-of-thought and heavy role-play hurt function accuracy ([Deflanderization](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13586v1)). SillyTavern's docs note that the model takes style and length "from the first message than anything else" ([SillyTavern](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/characterdesign)). Second, sampling-time suppression beats "don't say X". Negative instructions are weaker and can rebound ([arXiv 2601.08070](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08070v1)). The Antislop sampler backtracks on 8,000+ banned patterns while keeping quality, and antislop-vllm puts it in front of any OpenAI-compatible server ([Antislop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061); [antislop-vllm](https://github.com/sam-paech/antislop-vllm)). llama-server already exposes DRY, XTC and string-level `logit_bias` ([llama.cpp server](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)). XTC was reported to make a 12B model ignore context facts, so it is risky for grounded lines ([PR #6335](https://github.com/oobabooga/text-generation-webui/pull/6335)). Third, **every prompt component reduces diversity and memory reduces it most**: less context gives more varied speech ([Chu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21102)). Fourth, persona drift is real on HermesVoice's model family. Over 100+ rounds, Gemma 3 27B and peers lost **41% of persona-specific token patterns**, independent of context length ([arXiv 2512.12775](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12775v2)). Re-sending the voice card near the end of the prompt, as SillyTavern's Author's Note does at depth 0, counters this ([Author's Note](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/authors-note/)). Fine-tuning is the strongest lever: PUBG Ally distilled a 2B model on how players actually talk ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/)). It is also the costliest, and the keeper rules out Claude-written targets. To evaluate voices, do not rely on an LLM judge alone. The best judges reach about 69% on who-said-it against 90.8% for humans ([PersonaEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10014)). An embedding distinction score or a small speaker classifier is cheaper and more honest.

### Grounded use of rich context means filtering by what the pawn would notice

The failures players report are grounding failures: body type, distance, rooms, raids that scrolled out of context, lodgers taken for colonists. RimWorld already computes most of an appraisal model. Thoughts carry mood offsets and causes, opinions are numbers, and events have severities. So importance can come from game numbers instead of an LLM rating call, an inference supported by OCC-style appraisal work such as Chain-of-Emotion ([PLOS One](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11086867/)). The CPDC grounding advice is to limit answers to supplied facts and allow "unsure" ([arXiv 2510.13586](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13586v1)). rimagent's change-first perception packet (`food_days 9→7→5 ↓`, plus a world diff since the last step) is a compact way to send trends rather than snapshots ([rimagent](https://github.com/zorrobyte/rimagent)). Event+ shows that active letters and quests must persist for the event's duration, not for N messages ([Event+](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3612632140)). No source measured how many facts a 27B can reliably use in one line, or which serialization (JSON, key:value or prose) contradicts least. HermesVoice will have to measure that itself.

### Bounded social consequences need a closed enum, C# magnitudes and honest results

Every bounded design found has the same shape: the model picks a label from a closed set, and the game owns the magnitude, duration, stacking and cooldown. RimTalk uses ±3 for one day, stacking to three. RimMind uses ±1/±2 mood for 0.2 days and opinion clamped to ±5 over 10 days, fading after 70% of the duration ([RimTalk](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk); [RimMind](https://github.com/RimWorld-RimMind-Mod)). Epic's UEFN LLM NPCs return developer-defined structs whose game-side variables "provide the guardrails" ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fortnite/drive-gameplay-with-structured-output-in-unreal-editor-for-fortnite)). A live deployment uses "probabilistic reply-chain decay" to stop NPC-to-NPC loops and grounds free text onto fixed behaviour IDs ([Bounded Autonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04703)). When an NPC's function call failed, it narrated the action as done anyway. The fix was to return success or failure to the model ([arXiv 2407.03460](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03460)). RimChat's "explicit notice" rule addresses the same gap from the player's side. AIColonists goes one step further: it offers only engine-verified options and re-checks them on the main thread before they become vanilla jobs ([AIColonists](https://github.com/ptown99/AIColonists)). Because style pressure degrades function-argument accuracy, the effect label is best chosen in a separate short pass over the finished line, or as the last field of a tiny enum grammar, not mixed into a heavily role-played generation ([arXiv 2510.13586](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13586v1)). Exploit risk is real wherever players can type: Where Winds Meet players convinced an NPC that the player's character was pregnant with his child ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/wuxia-mmo-where-winds-meet-is-full-of-ai-chatbot-npcs-and-people-are-doing-all-the-standard-obscene-stuff-to-them-i-made-him-think-that-my-character-was-pregnant-with-his-child)).

### Rich inner and outer life rests on a static bio, an evolving bio and witness-only memory

The most convincing systems keep inner state outside the LLM as numbers and structured fields, which RimWorld already simulates (needs, mood, thoughts, traits, opinions). The LLM narrates that state and only occasionally proposes changes to it. **The proven guard against drift is a fixed bio that is never rewritten, paired with a dynamic bio rewritten at intervals and reviewed as a diff** ([SkyrimNet](https://goncalo22.github.io/SkyrimNet-GamePlugin/Features/overview); [CHIM](https://nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/126330)), and the ACL 2026 framing of "dynamic persona coherence" separates stable identity from emotional state ([ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1336.pdf)). This is exactly HermesVoice's fixed-bio, evolving-bio split. Witness-only memory is the cheapest source of colonists seeing the same event differently, and RimWorld already knows who was in the room. Smallville shows that gossip falls out of retrieval: a speaker mentions what it remembers, and the listener stores it. Tagging such memories "heard from A" with reduced confidence makes rumours traceable, and lets them feed a vanilla opinion thought. A per-pair Relationship, Feeling and Summary-Events record ([AGA](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02053v2)) maps directly onto RimWorld's opinion pairs. No system found models secrets explicitly, which leaves an open opportunity for a per-memory visibility flag. inZOI's visible thought stream suggests an inspect-pane monologue, which costs no voice time. Diaries (CHIM, SkyrimNet, Pawn Diary, Lucid Chronicle) are a proven idle-time job.

### Low latency comes from prefill reuse and pacing, not from speculation

In llama.cpp the large wins are prompt-prefix reuse and continuous batching. `cache_prompt` is on by default; the client can pin each stream to a slot with `id_slot`; a host-RAM prompt cache (`--cache-ram`, 8192 MiB by default) saves idle slots and restores evicted prefixes ([server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md); [PR #16391](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/16391)). Maintainers recommend client-side mapping of slots to prompts rather than trusting similarity-based slot assignment ([discussion #13606](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/13606)). Two caveats bite HermesVoice directly. **Gemma's sliding-window attention makes KV shifting "not possible"**, so `--cache-reuse`-style partial reuse fails, and some reuse needs `--swa-full` once prompts exceed the window ([particula.tech](https://particula.tech/blog/prompt-reprocessing-swa-hybrid-models-kv-cache); [discussion #13606](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/13606)). And **ngram speculation measured 88.3 vs 88.2 tok/s on "Gemma 4 26B MoE" for varied chat**, about 0% gain. The tester warned that repeated test prompts would have shown a false 4.75x ([speculative.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/docs/speculative.md); [DEV benchmark](https://dev.to/defilan/i-tested-speculative-decoding-on-my-home-gpu-cluster-heres-why-it-didnt-help-3ej6)). No public benchmark of MTP or draft-model speculation on short Gemma dialogue lines was found. Grammar-constrained JSON cost roughly 15 to 25% throughput in 2024 measurements ([DOMINO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06988)), so a tiny enum grammar is preferable to general JSON. On the game side, practitioners say players will not wait 3 s, aim for under 500 ms, and play an authored fallback on a miss ([DEV](https://dev.to/maximilian32541spec/how-we-built-dynamic-npc-dialogue-with-llms-lessons-from-early-access-4bfe)). The RimWorld mods, meanwhile, hide latency with ambient pacing and expiry: RimTalk waits 4 s between replies and drops stale requests. AIColonists' dual-clock gate (a tick interval plus a real-seconds ceiling) means "paused time costs exactly zero" and 3x/4x speed cannot flood the GPU ([AIColonists](https://github.com/ptown99/AIColonists)). No mod publishes measured end-to-end latency.

## No mod ships an engine, so one click is wide open

**Every RimWorld and Skyrim LLM mod found asks users to install and start Ollama, LM Studio, KoboldCpp or the Player2 app themselves**, then type a base URL and a model name ([RimTalk](https://github.com/jlibrary/RimTalk); [Mantella install](https://art-from-the-machine.github.io/Mantella/pages/installation.html)). Ranked from visible complaints, the failures are: JSON breakage on small models, URL mistakes (RimTalk's own guide wants no `/v1` for Ollama), the model not loaded yet, role drift, and VRAM squeezed by the game ([Local RimTalk guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3779985741)). Player2 is the nearest thing to one click. It is a desktop app on `localhost:4315` with no player keys, a `/v1/health` heartbeat that returns a version, and per-player credits. No source confirms that its inference runs locally ([Player2 API](https://blog.player2.game/p/building-ai-npcs-with-player2-api)). RimTalk, EchoColony and RimMind all integrate it, so players expect it as an option.

llama.cpp now does most of the fitting itself. `--fit` is on by default: it shrinks context to a floor (`--fit-ctx`, 4096 by default), then moves tensors to RAM, MoE experts first, keeping a per-device margin (`--fit-target`, 1024 MiB by default). **Passing `-ngl`, `--tensor-split` or `--override-tensor` silently disables it** ([server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md); [discussion #18049](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/18049)). `llama-fit-params` prints the chosen arguments without loading anything. Router mode adds LRU unloading and `--sleep-idle-seconds`. Ollama shows the model-download side: it picks context by VRAM tier and unloads after 5 idle minutes ([Ollama](https://docs.ollama.com/context-length); [FAQ](https://docs.ollama.com/faq)). Packaging favours a small default. The official Windows Vulkan build is **39.1 MB against 226 to 358 MB for CUDA plus a roughly 400 MB runtime** ([llama.cpp releases](https://api.github.com/repos/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases?per_page=1)). Unsigned binaries hit SmartScreen, and PyInstaller launchers draw antivirus false positives ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/smartscreen-reputation); [discuss.python.org](https://discuss.python.org/t/pyinstaller-false-positive/43171)). For lifecycle, no surveyed mod both auto-starts and reliably auto-stops its server. A Skyrim launcher admits it cannot close on game exit ([Nexus 119819](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/119819)). The robust primitives are a Windows Job Object with `KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE` ([Old New Thing](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20131209-00/?p=2433)) and Linux `PR_SET_PDEATHSIG` plus a parent-PID poll ([man7](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/PR_SET_PDEATHSIG.2const.html)). Steam `%command%` wrappers miss GOG players ([Arch Wiki](https://wiki.archlinux.org/title/Launch_option)). Whether Proton's container gives Windows-build players a separate localhost is unconfirmed. RimWorld's native Linux build sidesteps the question.

For HermesVoice's model, the GGUF sizes set the tiers. **Gemma 4 26B-A4B is 17.04 GB at Q4_K_M, 13.02 GB at Q3_K_M and 9.66 GB at IQ2_XXS** ([bartowski](https://huggingface.co/bartowski/google_gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-GGUF)). Moving MoE experts to the CPU with `--n-cpu-moe` reportedly took a 35B-A3B model on an 8 GB laptop GPU from about 4 to about 33 tok/s ([windowsforum recap](https://windowsforum.com/news/llama-cpp-n-cpu-moe-run-qwen3-35b-faster-on-8gb-rtx-laptop-gpus.449607/?amp=1)). An A4B MoE is therefore the right shape for 12 to 16 GB cards with ample system RAM, but not for 8 GB cards with 16 GB of RAM. Gemma 4 E4B (4.98 GB at Q4_K_M) is the same-family fallback ([unsloth](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/gemma-4-E4B-it-GGUF)). Gemma 4 thinking is controlled by a `<|think|>` token, and "disabled" replies on the larger models still carry an empty thought block, so the client must strip it. The Test button pattern is settled practice. Normalize the URL; call `GET {base}/models` to prove reachability and auth and fill a picker; send a chat completion with `max_tokens` around 8; read llama-server's `/health`, where 503 means loading, not failure ([Open WebUI](https://docs.openwebui.com/getting-started/quick-start/connect-a-provider/starting-with-openai-compatible.txt); [DEV smoke test](https://dev.to/seven7763/the-3-step-smoke-test-i-use-for-any-openai-compatible-api-311g)).

## Gap analysis and recommendations for HermesVoice

The table measures each of the keeper's goals against the late-2026 state of the art and names what HermesVoice must add. The prose after it covers the fixed decisions and their risks.

| Keeper goal | Best existing practice | Where the field still falls short | HermesVoice recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct, non-flowery voices | Persona strings, 15–40 char lines, anti-positivity rules (RimMind); persona add-ons (Persona Director) | Same house style across roles; no sampler-level slop control in any RimWorld mod; no distinctiveness metric | Voice card per pawn: 3–6 example lines + 1–3 tics + one affirmative register line, built from traits and backstory; re-inject it at the end of every prompt; `n_predict` cap + newline stop; DRY ~0.8; `logit_bias` bans on single-token slop; antislop backtracking for phrases if latency allows; XTC off; 2–4 memories max per line; measure an embedding distinction score per release |
| Rich use of RimWorld context | Toggleable full-pawn context (RimTalk), event persistence (Event+) | Grounding errors (body type, range, rooms); count caps instead of token budgets; ambient loops | Witness and hearing filter (room + range × Hearing), event ledger kept alive for each event's duration, trend packets (rimagent), explicit body/gear facts, strip ambient state before persisting, keep the trigger with each past line, game-number importance |
| Speech that loops into social consequences | Closed tags → ThoughtDefs with clamps (RimTalk, RimMind) | Effects off by default or uncalibrated; no feedback to the model; drastic actions in some mods | `{line}` then a separate 1–3 token intent pass from a closed enum; C# owns the magnitudes (start with RimMind-scale opinion ±5 over ~10 days with decay, mood ±1–3 for under a day, stack caps, per-pair daily caps); return applied/refused to the agent; only explicit, game-validated effects count; heard-from gossip as reduced-confidence memories |
| Rich inner and outer life per pawn | Daily first-person summaries (Lucid Chronicle), diaries, memory add-ons | All stateless in-game; no persistent per-pawn agent; no secrets | Fixed bio + evolving bio updated at importance thresholds and logged as a diff; per-pair relationship records; witness-only first-person memories; visibility flags (private/told-X/public); inspect-pane inner monologue; idle-time diaries and reflection |
| Low latency | One in flight + typed expiry (RimTalk); dual-clock gates (AIColonists); calm 3× / pause only for danger (rimagent) | No published latency figures; VRAM contention with the game | Byte-stable prefixes per slot; `id_slot` pinning; `--cache-ram` sized for the cast; measure effective prefill ((prompt_n+cache_n)/prompt_s) and `cache_n` on every call; expiry classes; never pause the game for speech; non-LLM conversation termination |
| An incredibly easy engine | None ship one; Player2 is closest | URL errors, model not loaded, JSON breakage, VRAM | Mod-owned child engine with Job Object / PDEATHSIG; Vulkan default + optional CUDA; tiered download with an E4B fallback; health-polled "warming up" light; three-step Test button; Player2 as a third provider |

**Port 8765 is a live conflict, not a theoretical one.** RIMAPI, Antfarm and zorrobyte/rimbridge all bind it, and a RIMAPI user is exactly the kind of player who would install HermesVoice. The bridge should try a different default and fall back across a small range. It should write the chosen port and a per-run token to a file the agent reads, and answer a `/health` that names itself and its version (Player2's heartbeat pattern), so the agent never talks to someone else's bridge by accident. Copy RIMAPI's useful parts (frame-drained queue, event stream) but not its security posture: bind to loopback only, require the token, and send no CORS headers. Run the drain on a `Root.Update` postfix with a per-frame time budget, as rimagent does, so `/state` works while paused and in menus. Give every POST /say a client operation id and drop expired operations server-side, since rimworld-mcp shows timed-out commands executing twice. Put the game tick in every reply, as RimBridgeServer does. For colony-event dial-out, back the push with a cursor (`/events?since=N`, as in rimbridge). Then a Hermes restart or a dropped connection replays the injury, death or raid it missed instead of losing it.

**Gemma 26B A4B is a sound choice; speculative decoding on it is the risk to measure.** RimTalk's own default is this model, which validates the choice. But the only measurement on this model family found about 0% from ngram speculation on varied chat. Lines of 15 to 40 tokens give any drafter little to work with. At A4B, decode is already cheap, and time-to-line is dominated by prefill on a cache miss. Benchmark MTP or draft speculation on real colonist lines (not repeated test prompts), and keep it only if it wins. The bigger lever is prefill reuse, and here the slot-leasing design meets Gemma's sliding window. When a pawn slot is leased to a new pawn, everything after the shared system block changes, so the slot pays a cold prefill. The keeper rejected building saved KV states per pawn, and that decision can stand. llama.cpp's host-RAM prompt cache already saves idle slot states and restores a matching prefix at no build cost, *provided the prefix is byte-identical*. So lay prompts out from most to least stable: world rules, then the fixed bio and voice card, then the evolving bio, then relations, then volatile state, then the request. Change the evolving bio only at reflection points. Keep timestamps out of the prefix. Then verify `cache_n` on re-leases and test `--swa-full` once prompts pass the sliding window. With three concurrent streams, KV per slot multiplies, so the engine's context balancer must budget three windows, not one.

**Two separate pawn agents are right for inner life, but they will not by themselves make the pawns sound different.** Separate slots give each pawn its own memories, opinions and secrets, which one model playing both sides cannot do. Literature on speaker distinction shows the shared weights impose a common style, so the voice card, sampler controls and example lines carry the distinctiveness. Each slot only appends the other's last line, so the conversation prefix grows append-only and stays cache-friendly. End conversations with non-LLM rules: a turn cap, a continuation probability that decays each turn (in the spirit of Bounded Autonomy), repetition detection, and pawns separating or changing jobs (RimTalk's walk-away complaint). Do not leave the decision to the model. Let only adjacency-pair openers (a question, an insult, a request) oblige a reply.

**The narrator slot should restrain itself the way the popular storytellers do.** Use RimGPT's skip-if-nothing-new rule, EchoColony's minimum gap with a bypass for deaths and major threats, and the rule that commentary can never fire events. If the narrator ever proposes incidents, follow Rimteller: choose from the triggerable vanilla incidents and leave self-driven events alone. The narrator is also the natural owner of idle-time work, such as colony chronicles and pawn diaries, which the audience demonstrably subscribes to.

**Keep reflexes in the engine and speech off the tick path.** rimagent, AIColonists, RWMind, PUBG Ally and the HN author ("LLM as merely an orchestrator") converge on this independently. HermesVoice should never pause the game for a line. It should drop lines past their expiry (urgent within seconds, chitchat within about 20 s, events within a few thousand ticks), and gate generation on both game ticks and real seconds so 3x/4x speed cannot flood the card. Effects should be applied only after a main-thread liveness check, because a pawn may have died or left while the line was generating.

**The one-click engine should be a child of RimWorld, not a service.** On the first main-menu load, the mod starts a small signed native launcher. On Windows the launcher holds a Job Object with kill-on-close; on Linux it uses PDEATHSIG plus a parent-PID poll. The engine dies with the game, crashes included. It starts with a Vulkan llama-server by default and offers an opt-in CUDA download when an NVIDIA card is detected. The installer probes VRAM and RAM and picks a download from the GGUF sizes: 26B-A4B Q4/Q5 fully on a 24 GB card; Q3/Q4 with experts partly on the CPU at 12 to 16 GB; Gemma 4 E4B below that, keeping the same family and chat template. There is one integration risk to flag. The hive ctx balancer and llama.cpp's `--fit` must not fight: any explicit `-ngl` or `-ot` turns fit off. Either the balancer owns placement entirely, computing its margin from measured non-game VRAM, or it delegates to `--fit` with `--fit-target` set to RimWorld's measured reserve. The Test button should normalize the URL; on a 404, retry once with `/v1` added or removed; list models; time an 8-token completion with thinking off; and treat a 503 as amber "loading" with a spinner, never red. Red results should name the cause in one line ("nothing listening on host:port", "key rejected", "wrong base URL; did you mean …/v1?"), and the raw request and response should sit in a copyable pane, as in RimTalk's API log. List Player2 as a third provider, because players already expect it.

**Coexist with RimTalk rather than ignore its 142k players.** RimMind ships a dedicated Bridge-RimTalk for exactly this reason. At minimum, detect RimTalk and suppress its bubbles for any pawn that a HermesVoice slot currently inhabits, so a pawn never speaks with two minds.

## Conclusion

The research suggests a split. In-game, prompt-per-event dialogue mods have reached their ceiling. Their users keep adding memory, persona and event persistence through add-ons, because a stateless prompt cannot hold a life. HermesVoice's external persistent agent is the architectural answer the add-on stack is reaching toward. Its advantage is not model quality, since RimTalk already runs the same Gemma. Its advantage is that identity, memory and opinions can accumulate and be curated outside the game. The evidence also bounds that ambition. Every system that let the LLM decide magnitudes or act freely drifted into embellishment or exploits, and no LLM colony manager beat vanilla in RLE's small runs. The defensible product is therefore a companion that remembers and reacts, with a narrow, calibrated channel into the simulation, not an agent that plays the colony.

The open questions are empirical and cheap to answer before building further. Does speculation help on 15 to 40 token lines with this MoE? Does the host-RAM cache actually restore a pawn's prefix on re-lease under sliding-window attention? Does a voice card with samplers separate pawns on an embedding distinction score? What opinion magnitude feels consequential without wrecking relationships, given that no source reports player data on calibration? The first HermesVoice milestones should be measurements, and the one-click engine is where a mod can most easily stand out, because no competitor has built one.
